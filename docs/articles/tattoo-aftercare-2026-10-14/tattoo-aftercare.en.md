# Tattoo Aftercare: How to Care for a New Tattoo

A new tattoo needs gentle care while the skin repairs itself. Good tattoo aftercare starts with clean hands, the correct dressing instructions, careful washing and protection from friction, soaking and sunlight. The routine changes depending on whether your artist used a temporary dressing or an adhesive healing film. A little early tenderness can happen, but symptoms that spread or worsen deserve medical attention. This guide explains what to do at home and when to get help.

This is general information for decorative tattoos. Follow personal medical instructions from your healthcare professional, and ask your artist for written instructions for the dressing and products used on your skin.

## Before removing the dressing, identify what you have

A temporary covering and a medical adhesive film, sometimes called second skin, can have different removal and replacement schedules. Ask your artist for the product name, the removal time and what to do if the covering comes loose. A timing rule intended for another dressing may not apply to yours. Vermont's professional regulator specifically includes dressing removal instructions in its tattoo aftercare requirements. See its [Tattoo Aftercare Guidelines](https://outside.vermont.gov/dept/sos/office_professional_regulation/professions/tattooist_body_piercer/tattooist_aftercare_guidelines.pdf).

With an intact adhesive film, some fluid containing plasma, blood and excess ink can collect underneath. For Saniderm specifically, leaking means the seal has broken: follow the manufacturer's removal and replacement instructions rather than taping over a leak. Other products can have different directions. Read [Saniderm's guidance on fluid and leaking](https://faq.saniderm.com/knowledge-base/my-saniderm-is-filled-with-blood-what-should-i-do/).

Strong burning, blistering or a spreading rash needs advice; getting through the advertised wear time is secondary to assessing a possible reaction. Avoid pulling a stuck covering away forcefully.

## How to clean a new tattoo

Once the dressing is due to come off and your instructions call for uncovered care:

1. Wash your hands with soap and water.
2. Gently wash the tattoo with lukewarm water and a mild, fragrance-free cleanser. Use clean fingertips and a light touch.
3. Rinse away the cleanser and loose surface residue. Leave attached flakes and scabs alone.
4. Pat dry with a clean disposable paper towel or a clean towel reserved for you. Avoid rubbing.
5. Let the surface dry before applying the recommended aftercare product.

The [NHS Norfolk tattoo aftercare guide](https://www.fyinorfolk.nhs.uk/my-health/piercings-tattoos/) supports gentle washing, drying and using the ointment advised for your tattoo. Mayo Clinic recommends washing twice daily and avoiding a direct shower jet on the fresh tattoo. Ask for an adjustment if your dressing or skin condition requires a different routine; constant scrubbing is unhelpful. See [Mayo Clinic's tattoo care guidance](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/adult-health/in-depth/tattoos-and-piercings/art-20045067).

## Moisturize lightly and keep the product routine simple

For uncovered care, use a small amount of the mild moisturizer or aftercare product recommended for your tattoo. Spread it gently; avoid leaving a thick, wet coating. Follow your film instructions before putting any product underneath an adhesive dressing.

Dryness, itching and light flaking can develop as the surface heals. Let flakes and scabs release on their own. If a product produces a new rash or persistent stinging, stop using it and seek advice. [Cleveland Clinic's dermatologist guide](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/tattoo-aftercare) discusses moisturizing, peeling and differences in healing time.

Avoid adding an antibiotic cream simply as a precaution unless a clinician recommends it. Topical antibiotics can cause contact dermatitis, and many minor wounds do not need them, according to the [American Academy of Dermatology's antibiotic guidance](https://www.aad.org/news/when-to-use-antibiotics-skin). Suspected infection needs medical assessment rather than a trial of leftover medication.

## Showering, swimming, exercise and sleep

**Showering:** Use a gentle, brief wash once permitted by your dressing instructions. Avoid scrubbing and prolonged soaking. A waterproof film does not make swimming during healing a good idea.

**Swimming and baths:** Stay out of pools, hot tubs, lakes and other bodies of water while the tattoo heals. Wait until the skin is healed, with no open areas, drainage or remaining scabs; ask your artist or clinician if you are uncertain. Mayo Clinic advises avoiding swimming during healing rather than treating a calendar date as clearance.

**Exercise:** Avoid activities that rub, stretch or repeatedly press on the area, and pause workouts that produce heavy sweating while it is healing. A waistband over a hip tattoo and a shared gym bench against a back tattoo create different problems. Your artist can help you plan around the placement. Ventura County's [recommended tattoo aftercare](https://rmadocs.venturacounty.gov/environmental-health/programs/body-art/publications/sample-body-art-recommended-tattoo-aftercare.pdf) includes activity restrictions and advises against rubbing alcohol or hydrogen peroxide on the tattoo.

**Sleep and clothing:** Choose clean, loose clothing and fresh bedding. Reduce pressure on the area. If fabric sticks, gently dampen it to release it instead of pulling it away dry. Avoid repeatedly rewrapping the tattoo unless instructed. These practical measures are described in [Ink Society Collective's aftercare guide](https://inksocietycollective.com/blogs/journal/how-to-care-for-a-new-tattoo); its separate product schedule is a studio protocol, so use the instructions for your own tattoo.

## Protect it from sunlight during healing and afterward

While the skin is healing, use shade and clean, loose clothing to keep direct sunlight off the area. Avoid putting sunscreen on raw or unhealed tattooed skin unless your clinician directs you to do so.

After healing, use a broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher when the tattoo is exposed. Reapply at least every two hours and follow its label. The [American Academy of Dermatology's tattooed skin guidance](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/tattoos/caring-for-tattooed-skin) also recommends avoiding tanning beds and checking changing tattooed skin with a dermatologist.

## Normal healing changes and warning signs

Mild early soreness, swelling, small amounts of clear fluid, and later itching or flaking can occur. Watch the direction of change: an area that is becoming more painful or spreading beyond its original boundary needs attention.

**Get prompt medical care for:**

- Redness or discoloration that spreads or darkens.
- Increasing pain, warmth or swelling.
- Pus, open sores, a bad smell or red streaks extending away from the tattoo.
- Fever, chills or feeling unwell alongside a worsening tattoo.

The [American Academy of Dermatology's guide to tattoo skin reactions](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/tattoos/tattoo-skin-reactions) recommends seeing a doctor promptly for infection symptoms. A photograph or a message to a studio cannot reliably establish the cause.

Redness can be harder to see on brown or black skin. Heat, swelling, pain and changes from your usual skin appearance also matter. Get emergency help for breathing difficulty, fainting, confusion or rapidly worsening illness. See the [NHS guidance on cellulitis and emergency symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cellulitis/).

Tell the clinician when the tattoo was done and which dressings or products you used. Notify your artist and ask for the ink brand, color and batch information if a reaction occurs. The [FDA's tattoo safety advice](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/think-you-ink-tattoo-safety) explains why those details can help an investigation.

## Common tattoo aftercare questions

### How long does a tattoo take to heal?

Expect a process over weeks, with deeper recovery potentially taking longer. Size, placement and skin response affect it. A tattoo looking better on the surface does not establish that every activity is safe. Use your skin's condition and professional advice, especially if symptoms persist.

### Can I scratch or remove peeling skin?

Leave attached skin and scabs alone. If itching becomes intense, a rash appears or symptoms worsen, get advice rather than increasing scratching or experimenting with new products.

### What should I do if the tattoo looks infected?

Contact a healthcare professional promptly. Keep the artist informed, but avoid waiting for a studio reply when symptoms are spreading, you have fever or you feel ill. Use emergency services for severe symptoms.

An AI tattoo prompt works best when it reads like a compact creative brief. The goal is to describe a coherent design, not to stack as many visual keywords as possible.

## Use a dependable prompt structure

Build the prompt from the largest decision to the smallest detail:

1. Main subject
2. Tattoo style
3. Composition and silhouette
4. Body placement
5. Line and shading treatment
6. Mood or visual energy
7. Details to exclude

A practical starting pattern is:

> A subject in a chosen tattoo style, arranged in a clear composition for a specific body area, with a defined line treatment and shading approach, creating a particular mood, without unwanted details.

You can leave out any part that does not matter to the concept. Specificity should clarify the image, not make the prompt longer for its own sake.

## Fine-line prompts

Fine-line concepts benefit from restraint. Mention a clean silhouette, deliberate negative space, limited crosshatching, and consistent thin lines. Keep the number of small symbols low, especially for a design intended at a modest size.

Example direction: a single peony with two leaves, fine-line tattoo style, vertical forearm composition, delicate contour lines, open negative space, minimal dot shading, calm and elegant, no frame and no text.

## Blackwork prompts

For blackwork, describe solid black shapes, bold contrast, and readable negative space. If you want texture, specify where it appears so the focal point remains clear.

Example direction: a raven in bold blackwork, wings forming a compact diamond silhouette for the upper arm, solid black feathers balanced with open skin breaks, high contrast, no color and no background scenery.

## Illustrative and engraving prompts

Illustrative work can support more narrative detail. Engraving-inspired designs use controlled hatching and an old-print character. Describe the focal hierarchy so texture does not overwhelm the subject.

Example direction: a lighthouse above storm waves, engraving-inspired tattoo illustration, oval composition for the calf, crisp outer contour, fine directional hatching, dramatic but readable, no lettering.

## Ornamental and geometric prompts

Symmetry, repetition, and spacing are central to ornamental designs. Name the axis, overall shape, and density. For geometric work, ask for precise alignment while keeping enough negative space for the pattern to remain legible.

Example direction: an ornamental crescent with botanical filigree, symmetrical sternum composition, balanced thin and medium line weights, generous spacing, elegant and ceremonial, no realistic shading.

## Add placement early

Placement should influence the concept from the beginning. A narrow forearm, curved shoulder, flat upper back, and wraparound ankle each ask for a different silhouette. Include the body area and orientation before adding decorative detail.

AI cannot judge your anatomy from text alone. Treat placement language as a compositional guide, then ask your tattoo artist to fit the design to your body.

## Use exclusions with purpose

Exclusions are useful when the model repeatedly adds something you do not want. Keep them short and concrete: no text, no frame, no color, no extra flowers, no background scene. A long negative list can create conflicting instructions.

After the first result, refine the strongest version with small, measurable requests. More negative space, fewer leaves, heavier outer contour, or a narrower silhouette gives the next iteration a clear target.
